Base.com

Initial Strategic Assessment & Growth Approach

1.Overall ambition

Accelerate revenue growth by maximising the value of existing customers, improving retention and accelerating the activation and growth of new customers.

My initial thinking is built around one central question

How can we generate more value from our existing customer base while making the business more scalable and better positioned to acquire and grow the right customers?

I would approach this through a hypothesis-led process: understand what is working today, identify opportunities and bottlenecks, validate assumptions through data and customer conversations, and prioritise actions according to their potential business impact.

2.Initial business hypotheses and opportunities

H1Unlock additional revenue from existing customers

There may be untapped growth potential within the existing customer base.

I would investigate

  • Revenue contribution and evolution by customer.
  • Additional integrations, functionalities and services that could generate value.
  • Underutilised platform capabilities.
  • Customer growth ambitions and operational needs.

Objective: Identify and prioritise account expansion opportunities, with a clear understanding of their potential revenue contribution (from initial data gathered by Sales).

H2Improve customer adoption, retention and loyalty

Some customers may not fully exploit Base's capabilities.

I would analyse

  • Feature adoption, active usage.
  • Evolution of transaction volumes and revenue.
  • Recurring technical or operational issues.
  • Reasons behind churn and revenue contraction.

Objective: Increase the value customers get from Base, identify risks earlier and develop a more proactive approach to customer retention and growth.

H3Make bespoke implementations more scalable

Bespoke customer requirements can create value and differentiation, but they can also increase implementation time and complexity and cost.

I would work with the Implementation team to identify

  • Successful implementations and configurations.
  • Integrations and functionalities repeatedly requested.
  • Recurring customer requirements and operational workflows.
  • Developments that could be reused or adapted across customers.
  • Opportunities to package existing capabilities into repeatable solutions.

Objective: Create additional monetisation opportunities.

H4Accelerate customer activation and time to revenue

Getting a customer live is only the first milestone. The real objective is to get them to their expected operational volume as quickly as possible.

I would review

  • Time from contract signature to go-live.
  • Time to first transaction and full operational volume.
  • Recurring implementation and integration bottlenecks.
  • Technical dependencies and customer-side constraints.
  • Handover between Sales, Implementation and Customer Success.

Objective: Reduce time to value, improve the onboarding experience and accelerate revenue generation.

H5Use customer data to improve acquisition quality

Understanding the characteristics of our most successful customers can help refine the ICP and improve prospect qualification.

I would compare customers based on

  • Business maturity.
  • Revenue and growth trajectory.
  • Transaction volumes and product adoption.
  • Retention and expansion.
  • Operational complexity.

The objective is to identify common characteristics among the strongest-performing accounts and share these insights with Sales to support ICP refinement and better prospect qualification.

H6Combine technology with expertise

There may be an opportunity to strengthen Base's differentiation by combining its technological capabilities and operational data with deeper expertise.

Potential areas to explore include

  • Operational performance diagnostics.
  • Marketplace and inventory optimisation.
  • Identification of order and fulfilment bottlenecks.
  • Automation of repetitive operational tasks.
  • AI-assisted analysis, recommendations and workflows.
  • AI agents capable of executing authorised operational tasks through APIs and potentially MCP.

The longer-term ambition could be helping merchants understand, optimise and improve their performance.

3.Customer base segmentation and prioritisation

I would start by segmenting the existing customer base to understand where the greatest opportunities and risks lie.

The analysis would consider four dimensions

  • Current value: Revenue contribution and transaction volumes.
  • Growth potential: Expansion opportunities and business ambitions.
  • Customer health: Adoption, engagement and retention risks.
  • Operational complexity: Implementation effort, support requirements and cost to serve.

This would help identify four main customer profiles:

Customer segmentPriority
High-performing customersProtect relationships and identify further expansion opportunities.
High-growth-potential customersDevelop targeted plans to accelerate revenue and adoption.
Under-adopting customersIdentify usage gaps and improve the value they get from Base.
At-risk customersAddress declining performance and protect recurring revenue.

The objective is to move towards a more targeted customer strategy, allocating resources where they can generate the greatest commercial impact.

This segmentation would also provide valuable insights to Sales to help refine the ICP and improve acquisition quality.

4.How I would approach it

I see three complementary workstreams.

A. Internal diagnosis and cross-functional collaboration

Work closely with the different teams to understand business performance, customer needs and operational constraints.

Sales – Pierre and Thibault

  • Understand customer acquisition, segmentation and commercial performance.
  • Identify existing customer expansion opportunities.
  • Share insights from successful accounts to support ICP refinement.

Implementation – Cyril

  • Understand the onboarding journey and technical complexity.
  • Identify recurring bottlenecks and bespoke requirements.
  • Assess which configurations, integrations and developments can be reused.
  • Understand implementation effort, cost and resource constraints.

Customer Success – Juliette/Myself

  • Understand customer health, adoption and satisfaction.
  • Identify churn risks and revenue contraction.
  • Review how growth opportunities are identified and managed.
  • Connect customer feedback with commercial opportunities.

B. Direct customer engagement

Internal data will help identify opportunities, but it will not always explain why customers behave as they do.

I would meet customers across the different segments: high-performing accounts, customers with growth potential, under-adopting customers and customers experiencing operational difficulties.

Conversations would focus on

  • Their business objectives and growth ambitions.
  • Their experience with Base and what they value most.
  • Operational challenges and processes that remain manual.
  • What prevents them from growing faster.
  • Capabilities or services that could create additional value.
  • Where automation, integrations or AI could improve their operations.

The objective is to understand the underlying business problems, identify recurring patterns and opportunities that may not be visible through internal data alone.

C. From customer insights to scalable commercial opportunities

For each recurring customer need, I would assess

  1. Is this a problem shared by several customers?
  2. Can an existing capability/feature address it?
  3. Can the solution be reused or adapted?
  4. What would it cost to deliver and maintain?
  5. Does it improve retention, generate additional revenue or reduce cost to serve?
  6. Could it become part of a repeatable commercial proposition?

The objective is to connect customer experience, technical feasibility and commercial value rather than simply collect feedback or feature requests.

I would also introduce customer cohort analysis to understand revenue evolution over 3, 6 and 12 months, identify the strongest-performing segments and understand what differentiates successful customers.

Recurring revenue and non-recurring implementation should be tracked separately to ensure consistent performance measurement.

Targets would be established once the current baseline and data quality are understood.
